Aircraft overhaul · implemented in 0.19.0

This review exposes the full revised catalogs for all 14 starts. Three-year generations, the Japan 1936 exception, read-only shore aircraft, physical reinforcement and automatic air operations are implemented. Ranges are combat radii, not one-way ferry ranges. Generic scenario fits and approximate game values are identified; they are not claims of historical aircraft specifications.

  1. Naval aircraft: review a qualification fit about every three years, with fighter, strike and reconnaissance roles. Reuse the same representative airframe with a newer fit where history does not justify a new type. Never unlock a named aircraft before its service/development date.
  2. Japan, 1936: confirmed exception keeps the authored Hibari, Raiden, Tenzan and Shinden progression. Japan, 1922 follows the historical/period qualification sequence. This interpretation is confirmed.
  3. Shore aircraft: a separate, read-only government-managed catalog. Use representative maritime patrol and torpedo/anti-shipping types, with three-year generations, as confirmed. Identify the actual owning service: land-based does not automatically mean army-operated.
  4. Compatibility: explicit carrier-capable, catapult floatplane, flying boat and landplane labels. A patrol flying boat cannot fill a carrier fighter slot; a landplane cannot refuel by landing on a carrier. Only carrier-qualified models may use carriers as ferry stops.
  5. Support: dated depot and oiler families in 1922/1932/1942, with Japan's 1936 Standard Maru hybrid exception. Civilian merchant hulls remain in the register and grow outside player construction.

Operational air warfare: automatic state model

Admirals choose a broad search sector toward the current objective or last contact. Local daylight and one regional weather state govern searches and launches. A strike needs a recent contact, sufficient radius, aircraft, crews and supplies; it spends 35 minutes plus 0.3 minutes per strike aircraft and a training allowance assembling, travels out and back, then spends 90 minutes rearming after the return flight. These are provisional balance ranges. CAP stays explicit, escort fighters leave with the strike, and an exposed deck or weak CAP increases carrier risk. Reports explain the modifiers; no required sortie orders.

Scheduled transitions are processed by the existing simulation worker. Weather updates by region and time block, not per aircraft every minute. Aircraft and aviators must be conserved through losses, rescue, ferry transfers and recovery. Reconnaissance of anchorages must produce dated observations rather than reveal live enemy inventories.

Strategic bombing recommendation

Add later as an abstract government-run air campaign under Land campaigns. Model bomber strength, escorts, enemy fighters/AA, weather, industrial damage and repair, with representative bomber types in the passive catalog. Keep it separate from maritime aircraft and the naval aircraft budget. It should affect economic output gradually, with limits and repair, rather than repeatedly subtracting permanent GDP for every raid. Shipping losses and bombing damage must not charge the same industrial loss twice.

Reason: long-range bombers mattered, but maritime patrol and industrial bombing had different targets, equipment and operational constraints. First make the aircraft-to-base-to-shipping loop reliable, then add a weekly strategic bombing resolution with visible production disruption.

Primary references: USS Hornet action report; Midway weather report; Naval Aviation Museum: PBY-5; USAF Museum: strategic bomber technologies. These support the distinctions; timing and growth coefficients are game balance proposals.
