## 1. The prohibition — the only nation in this project whose treaty is not a bargain

⚠ **Every other divergence in this project is a nation inside the Washington system, defecting on a plan, from the day of signature. Germany is not in the room.** She is under the Treaty of Versailles, signed on 28 June 1919 and in force from 10 January 1920, and what Part V, Section II does to her is not a ratio — it is a list.

| | Versailles permits | Versailles forbids | The consequence for this scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital ships** | 6 battleships of the *Deutschland* or *Lothringen* type, 2 more in reserve (Art. 181) | anything newer; replacement before 20 years, or above **10,000 t** (Art. 190) | The newest capital ship Germany may own was designed in 1903 and was obsolete when *Dreadnought* commissioned. The earliest legal successor to *Hannover* is 1927, at 10,000 tons |
| **Cruisers** | 6 light cruisers, 2 in reserve | replacement above **6,000 t** | *Emden*, laid down 8 December 1921, is legal to the ton — and is the last cruiser Germany ever lays down |
| **Flotilla** | 12 destroyers, 12 torpedo boats | replacement above **800 t** / **200 t**, before 15 years | Two hundred and twenty-seven-ton boats are on the establishment because the Treaty counts hulls and not capability |
| **Submarines** | ⚠ **NONE. NOT ONE, OF ANY SIZE, EVER** (Art. 191) | construction, acquisition, and possession alike | **This is the whole divergence.** The thing Germany decides to build in 1920 is the one thing the Treaty names an absolute |
| **Naval aviation** | none (Art. 198) | military and naval air forces entire | An arm founded in the twenties predates the man who historically took it away, and both German naval aircraft need no aerodrome |
| **The staff** | — | the Great General Staff, dissolved and not reconstituted (Art. 160) | The finding of 1920 is adopted by a *navy*, not imposed by a state, and that is why it survives 1933 unchanged |

**So Germany cannot lie the way the others lie.** Britain lies about a displacement; America lies about a displacement and then about a rate; Japan lies about a registry. Each of those is a number on a form that somebody could in principle audit. ⚠ **Germany has no form to lie on, because she is permitted nothing to declare.** Her lie is about **existence**, and the only way to tell that lie is to have the thing built somewhere else, by somebody else, on somebody else's paperwork.

## 2. Road to 1935 — timeline

### 2.1 1918–1922 — the camp, the finding, and the office

**October 1918.** A submarine officer is taken prisoner and spends the rest of the war and a good deal of 1919 in a British camp, working out why the first tonnage war failed. He reaches the correct answer — **the convoy, not the depth charge** — which is the answer the Admiralty itself reached and did not publish.

**1919.** He comes home, and is taken into the Reichsmarine the following year. ⚠ **The divergence is not that he reaches that conclusion; he did. It is that the Reichsmarine adopts it as its official finding rather than as one officer's memorandum**, and organizes around it. The submarine faction wins the internal argument in 1922 and never loses it. **No personal patronage device is required and none is used** — the mechanism is navy-internal for eleven years, which is nearly what happened anyway.

**1921.** The Mackensen-class battlecruisers are broken up where they stand, and *Sachsen* and *Württemberg* with them, under Article 186. ⚠ **Every other navy's canceled hulls in this project were a choice. These were cut up by treaty at gunpoint, and nothing about them can be resumed.** The German capital-ship question is closed by somebody else, which is the precondition that makes the 1922 argument winnable at all.

**8 December 1921.** *Emden* is laid down at Wilhelmshaven as an Article 190 replacement, four percent complete when the Washington Treaty is signed two months later. **She is the only German keel on any slip on that day, and she is the last German cruiser.**

**1922. IvS opens in The Hague** — *Ingenieurskantoor voor Scheepsbouw*, a Dutch engineering office with German staff, German money and Dutch letterhead. Germany banks **designs, jigs, drawings and people, because she is not permitted to own plant.**

### 2.2 1922–1932 — the flat decade, and the hull that ends it

⚠ **THIS IS THE PART OF THE GERMAN STORY THAT IS ACTUALLY REMARKABLE, AND IT IS THE PART THAT LOOKS LIKE NOTHING.** `build_model_de.py` runs 1922–31 at **5,600 t-equivalent a year** — her real historical construction rate, and under a tenth of Japan's envelope and about a tenth of Britain's — and **it lays down one hull with it.** Everything else goes on a fleet she may not own.

**1926.** The first IvS boat is built in **Finland** as a private venture for a customer who never takes delivery; two more follow in **Spain**; a fourth line runs through **Turkey**. The type was never secret in the usual sense — the drawings were commercial, the yards foreign, the trials public. ⚠ **The only concealment that mattered was that no German yard cut a plate. What came home instead were the crews.**

**1926.** The marine diesel goes to sea in a Finnish-built boat before it goes to sea in a German one, which is the whole history of this navy in a sentence.

**1927.** The storage-battery line opens, funded on a commercial traction contract that was never entirely fictitious, and runs for nineteen years with no submarine to fit a cell to.

**1928 staff study — the ocean campaign.** The conclusion the surface officers disliked and never overturned: a boat's endurance is set not by its bunkers but by the distance to the nearest thing that can fill them, so every sea south of Biscay is closed to Germany **by geography rather than by the Royal Navy.** The answer put forward is a warship that carries the base with it.

**1929–30.** The raider–tender is drawn at the Hague office **under a Netherlands merchant number.**

**1931.** The valve laboratory has a working pulse set on a barge in the Kieler Förde, and a naval contract eighteen months later. ⚠ **This is the earliest radar line in the project and nobody expected it.** The surface officers regard it as a liability, on the grounds that anything which transmits is a beacon for something that does not. The submarine staff take the opposite view and take the sets.

**1932. The envelope moves for the first time in ten years — 5,600 t-equivalent to 9,000 — and the first raider keel goes down**, against a votes line for *fleet auxiliaries* that nobody has yet asked to see itemized. One has followed every year since, and 3 are on the slips at the end of 1935. A 19,000-ton hull is **more than two years of the entire German naval construction vote**, which is why it happens in 1932 and not in 1928.

### 2.3 1933–1935 — the money, the sections, and the announcement

**1933. The money arrives.** ⚠ **And that is the whole of its contribution: it arrives at a program that has already existed for eleven years.** The envelope goes from 9,000 t-equivalent to 22,000, then 48,000, then 84,000 — near-vertical, and the ordinary consequence of a large industrial country deciding to build submarines. **The extraordinary part happened in the decade before it.**

**1933. The envelope reaches 22,000, and the first 6 ocean boats are ordered** with a second raider. The ocean boat is drawn twice and the second drawing is the one that matters: *name the ocean, then draw the boat that crosses it and comes home with fuel to spare.* She is the first submarine in any navy specified with a search set on her mast.

**1934. The envelope reaches 48,000 and the coastal line opens: 27 coastal boats and 16 more ocean boats ordered, and not one of them commissioned.** Sections, jigs and machinery, at yards that have had the drawings for years. Nothing floats. This is the historical Mob-U program and it is the only way the following June works.

**June 1935. The reveal, and it is total.** ⚠ **The envelope reaches 84,000, and in one year Germany commissions 1 raider and 33 submarines — 27 coastal boats ordered in 1934 and 6 ocean boats ordered in 1933 — with 114 more on the slips behind them.** `build_model_de.py`'s counterfactual runs the identical envelope and the identical shares with ordinary lead times, and delivers **nothing at all** by the end of 1935: a yard that has never seen the drawing takes 3 years over a coastal boat and 5 over an ocean boat, where one that has had it since 1926 takes 1 and 2. **The thirteen flat years are not backstory. They are the delivery schedule**, and every naval attaché in Europe can do that arithmetic in an afternoon.

**June 1935. Britain legalises it unilaterally, without consulting the other Washington signatories.** ⚠ **This is the first visible crack in the room** and Germany did not make it — Britain did, in four pages of Admiralty file that are chiefly about tonnage ratios and eleven of Foreign Office file that are chiefly about the room.

**9–23 December 1935. Second London.** Germany is not invited and is the reason verification is on the agenda. §3.

## 3. Why Second London fails — the German view, from outside the door

Germany is not a party to Washington and not a delegate at London, and **she is nonetheless the reason the conference convenes on verification rather than on tonnage.** A signatory that has stopped behaving as though the room exists is a different problem from a signatory who is cheating inside it: cheating can be audited, and existence cannot be un-announced.

Every defector in the room arrives unable to accept an inspector, each for its own unrelated reason, and none of them knows about the other two (frame §1.4). What they can all agree on is that the German announcement is intolerable — and they discover, one delegation at a time, that **the only signatory with standing to have stopped it had already conceded it in June.** France tables verification as a precondition on 20 December and the United States seconds it on the 22nd; Japan refuses outright, Britain and Washington enter reservations that amount to the same answer, and the conference adjourns *sine die* on 23 December.

⚠ **Germany's exposure is six months old when the scenario opens, which makes her the freshest crisis on the board and the least resolved.** Every other divergence has had its reveal absorbed, argued about and filed. Hers is still moving.

## 4. The ledger at 1 January 1936

*There is no treaty ledger for Germany, because there is no treaty she is inside. What follows is the prohibition ledger: what Versailles permits, what she has, and what she has instead. Permitted figures are Versailles Part V, Section II; hull counts are the scenario's order of battle (`de-nothing-above-water-scenario.md` §3.1) and the envelope is its §3.2; displacements are the platform catalog's.*

| Category | Versailles permits | Actual | Reading | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Capital ships** | 6 + 2 reserve, *Deutschland* or *Lothringen* type | **8**, all of them, and not one modernized | **at the cap, and worthless** | Six in commission and two in reserve, the newest designed in 1903. Not one mark has been spent on any of them since 1922 |
| **Cruisers** | 6 + 2 reserve | **7** — *Emden* and six survivors of the Gazelle and Bremen classes | **under** | The Königsbergs, *Leipzig*, *Nürnberg* and all three Panzerschiffe were **never ordered**. That money is the divergence |
| **Destroyers / torpedo boats** | 12 + 12 | **12 + 12**, all prewar | **at the cap** | No German flotilla craft has been laid down since 1918 in this world. The boats exist so that officers can go to sea |
| **Submarines** | ⚠ **ZERO. THE TREATY NAMES IT AN ABSOLUTE** | **33** in commission — 27 coastal, 6 ocean — and **114 building** | ⚠ **∞** | Announced in June, in the water by autumn, and impossible without the thirteen years. There is no ratio to compute because the denominator is nought |
| **Raiders / tenders** | no category; they are declared *fleet auxiliaries* | **1** in commission, **3** building | **undeclarable** | 19,000 t apiece against a 10,000 t replacement cap she is not even claiming to be inside |
| **Naval aviation** | ⚠ **PROHIBITED ENTIRELY** (Art. 198) | **one type**, 615 km reach, no radar, and **one deck to fly it from** | ⚠ **the gap that is real** | Neither airplane needs an aerodrome, which is why a navy that owns none chose them. The one that reaches the middle of the ocean is four years away |
| **Construction vote** | not limited by treaty | **5,600 t-equiv/yr for ten years, then 9,000 · 22,000 · 48,000 · 84,000** | **a 15-fold step in four years** | And the ten flat years are where the fleet came from |

**Read the last two rows together.** Germany is at or under every hull cap Versailles wrote, has flagrantly and publicly broken the one it wrote as an absolute, and **is blind.** She has 33 submarines and one airplane type that can search 615 kilometers, and a wolfpack's binding constraint is finding a convoy and not sinking one.

⚠ **The hull counts are `build_model_de.py`'s and its rows are END OF YEAR.** Regenerate with `python build_model_de.py --emit`; the output is `data/derived/de_build_model.json`, and `python build_model_de.py --leads` prints the counterfactual that makes June 1935 arithmetic rather than assertion. The base fleet is `data/ships/de.json`, which the pack references and never modifies.
