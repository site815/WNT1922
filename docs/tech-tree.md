# Naval technology and organization tree

The same level descriptions appear in the game. All fourteen branches start at level 1 and end at level 9. Each upgrade requires the previous level; there are no hidden cross-branch prerequisites. Four projects may run together. Facility funding controls output, not an already funded expansion.

Level 1 is the opening baseline, including in 1936. Undated organizational programs can be funded from 1922. Dated technologies open level 2 in the listed first year; each subsequent upgrade opens three years later. No project may start before its availability year. Completing a dated upgrade represents a fleet-wide program, not an early unlock of catalog equipment or aircraft. Descriptions are provisional in-universe progression, not claims of identical historical adoption by every navy.

Upgrade gold and industry costs are base cost × (1 + 0.35 × completed upgrades), rounded up. Influence and duration are fixed per branch. Funding commits the whole project cost; operating funding for facilities is separate. Bonuses use completed upgrades (level − 1). Training and logistics gains are capped by their stat limits. Damage resistance divides incoming damage by 1 + 0.06 × upgrades; it does not subtract six percentage points. Repair bonuses from logistics and damage control add together.

## Suggested future branches

- Naval medicine and survivor recovery: improve treatment and shorten recovery delays. Keep it distinct from damage control, which prevents and limits shipboard casualties.
- Operational communications: radio discipline, message security and command delays could create a useful branch once communication delays are simulated.
- Avoid a separate convoy logistics or pilot-training doctrine panel for now: the existing supply, ASW, aviation and school branches already cover those functions.

## Historical grounding

The progression uses period practices as inspiration; game levels and numerical bonuses are provisional balance. Useful primary references are the [US Navy Combat Information Center manual (RADSIX)](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/c/cic--combat-information-center-manual-radsix.html), the [Naval History and Heritage Command ASW overview](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/museums/nmusn/explore/photography/wwii/wwii-atlantic/battle-of-the-atlantic/anti-submarine-warfare.html), and [CIC Yesterday and Today](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/c/cic-combat-information-center-yesterday-today.html).
