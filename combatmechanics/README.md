# Shared naval combat

The campaign and Tactical Engagements use the same deterministic ten-second engine. A normal campaign tick remains 15 minutes and advances 90 combat steps. Quick resolution runs those exact steps without rendering; watching does not grant different damage rolls or alter the campaign RNG. Ten seconds provides room for gun reloads, shell flight, torpedo travel, turns and damage control that one 15-minute damage roll cannot show. The model rounds weapon timing up to that fixed step; it does not simulate individual crew actions or ballistic fluid dynamics.

Edit `rules.mjs` for weapon, damage, aviation, timing and recording coefficients; `ship-ai.mjs` for doctrines, targeting and formations; `campaign-rules.mjs` for decisive-action thresholds and compatibility scheduling; `scenarios.mjs` for the historical setups. These are direct JavaScript source assets, bundled alongside the other game files. Changing them does not require rebuilding Unreal or cooking the ship models. Restart the game after editing. A packaged update still needs the normal release build to redistribute the edited files.

`engine.mjs` exposes `createCombat`, `advanceCombat`, `resolveCombat`, `combatSnapshot`, `combatSummary` and a future-facing `setCombatOrders`. The current player flow selects fleets, doctrine, formation and environment before starting; it does not expose live tactical orders. `index.mjs` and the engine are browser compatible. Campaign inventory adapters have their own files so the standalone simulator does not depend on campaign workers.

Coordinates are kilometres east (`x`) and north (`y`), with headings clockwise from north in degrees, speeds in knots and clocks in seconds. Stable ship IDs are `A:groupId:hullIndex` or `B:groupId:hullIndex`. A hull owns health, machinery, fire control, fires, flooding, shell/torpedo/depth-charge stores and aircraft. Gunnery uses range, target size and aspect, armour, visibility, radar, sea state, crew quality and doctrine. Actual projectiles delay impacts until arrival. Sinking follows lethal damage or progressive fire/flooding; damaged ships can withdraw and leave the encounter. Submarines use submerged speed and torpedoes; escort AI closes for a bounded sonar/depth-charge model. It does not model detailed underwater acoustics, per-turret armour zones, fuel explosions, individual compartments or a historical fire-control computer.

Carrier flights are grouped moving units with strike/fighter counts. CAP, local AA, attack probability and remaining aircraft determine their damage. A flight can recover aboard a surviving friendly carrier with deck room. Aircraft counts are conserved through launch, casualties, carrier sinking and diversion. Campaign carrier aircraft are held in existing sortie inventory while the tactical encounter owns them: they cannot also launch a strategic sortie or be replenished at port. On completion, aboard survivors return to their actual recovery carriers; flights still airborne at a scenario time limit use normal campaign return/diversion rules.

New fleet engagements, convoy interceptions, maritime aircraft attacks and naval attacks on anchorages use this engine. A coastal battery is a stationary `FORT` logical unit, explicitly hidden from the ship-model renderer; it is not represented by a fake ship. Merchant classes use the existing campaign merchant abstraction (5,000-ton representative tactical hulls); actual merchant GRT and economic losses still come from the campaign's shipping register. Coastal facility damage, land bombing, the economic cost of raids and strategic transit remain campaign systems. Older saved aggregate air/shore battles retain their original aggregate resolution through completion; their missing tactical history is never reconstructed. Old active surface/convoy records transition at the next exchange and mark that transition in metadata.

The campaign adapter applies exact surviving hull counts, health and finite torpedo stores through the established sailor/aircrew rescue, repair, war-score and merchant ledgers. Nation training, morale, crew coverage, fleet supply and relevant gunnery/radar/damage-control research modify the actual tactical inputs. Concurrent air attacks synchronize existing hull damage and actual available CAP; a surface update cannot resurrect a ship sunk by a separate attack. The campaign winner follows the shared combat winner, including disengagement.

Records retain actual positions and timed attack, impact, withdrawal and sinking events. Recording is bounded to 120 pose frames, 1,800 events and about 340 KB per action, with coarser sampling for large fleets. Omitted poses/events set `history.truncated` and `omittedEvents`; no missing attack is inferred. Completed campaign tactical records share a 1 MB archive budget. Older records can lose tactical playback while retaining their aggregate losses. Very large actions may have too little retained track history for a complete movie; current simulation state remains available. Replay camera cuts are presentation only.

These are transparent gameplay approximations, not validated historical accuracy. The three presets use selected principal ships, rounded engineering values, assumed initial geometry and existing historical ship models. Outcomes are generated, never scripted to guarantee a famous sinking. Midway omits escorts, land-based aircraft and the later submarine attack; North Cape omits the wider destroyer and convoy forces. Existing battle, morale, resource, convoy, personnel and aircraft tests cover the surrounding campaign effects.
